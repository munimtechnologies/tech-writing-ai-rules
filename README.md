# tech-writing-ai-rules

What do the people who pay for or accept technical writing say about AI?

This repository holds fourteen pre-registered studies: ten are graded, and studies 11 to 14 are pre-registered and waiting for their runs. Each reads its sources' own pages, keeps every AI sentence or answer as found, and codes them against a rubric fixed in advance. It also ships five agent skills: [one built on studies 2 and 3](#agent-skill-check-a-projects-ai-rule-before-contributing), [one built on studies 7 and 8](#agent-skill-check-microsoft-learn-ai-labels), [one built on study 10](#agent-skill-check-a-docs-pages-markdown-copy), [one built on a bug study 10 led to](#agent-skill-check-a-hugo-sites-markdown-layouts), and [one that measures HTML in Markdown copies](#agent-skill-measure-html-in-a-markdown-copy) (study 14 uses it), and a practical guide built on studies 7 to 10: [labelling AI-made docs pages so people and agents both see the label](guides/label-ai-made-docs.md). Where a finding points to a small fix in someone else's docs, we offer it as a disclosed pull request or bug report ([fixes and bug reports offered upstream](#fixes-and-bug-reports-offered-upstream)).

1. **Paid technical-writing programs** (graded 2026-11-03): many software companies and developer publications pay $100 to $900 per tutorial or how-to. Community lists give each program's pay and topics, but not its rule on AI-written or AI-assisted work.
2. **Open-source AI contribution policies and documentation** (graded 2026-11-05): do projects' AI rules say whether they cover docs, READMEs, tutorials and translations, or only code?
3. **Separate documentation repos** (graded 2026-11-07): when a project keeps its docs in a repo of their own, do that repo's contributor files show the project's AI rule?
4. **Documentation style guides** (graded 2026-11-07): what do 19 public style guides say about writing with AI, writing for AI, and writing about AI?
5. **Docs for AI readers** (graded 2026-11-08): do the 56 projects with AI contribution rules serve llms.txt files, Markdown copies or robots.txt blocks to AI readers of their docs, and does that differ between projects that ban AI-made contributions and projects that allow them?
6. **What llms.txt files tell AI agents to do** (graded 2026-11-10): across the open llms-txt-hub directory, how many llms.txt files give AI agents directives, and how often do those directives promote the site, ask the agent to act (install, call, pay, report), or try to override the agent? See [Result 6](#result-6-what-do-llmstxt-files-tell-ai-agents-to-do-read-2026-11-09-graded-2026-11-10).
7. **How much of Microsoft Learn is labelled as made with AI?** (graded 2026-11-09): Microsoft asks its docs authors to mark Markdown "created or substantially modified with AI assistance" with an `ai-usage` front matter entry (`ai-assisted` or `ai-generated`). It [plans to retire most of its public docs repos by the end of December 2026](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/skills-hub-blog/changes-to-microsoft-learn%e2%80%99s-public-documentation-repositories/4554909), after which they "will no longer be publicly viewable". Before they close, this census counted the labels in the 30 largest public MicrosoftDocs repos. See [Result 7](#result-7-how-much-of-microsoft-learn-is-labelled-as-made-with-ai-read-and-graded-2026-11-09).
8. **Does Microsoft Learn tell its readers, people and agents, that an article was made with AI?** (graded 2026-11-10): Microsoft promises that every article with AI-generated content includes "text acknowledging the role of AI" at its end. For a seeded sample of labelled and unlabelled articles, this study read each live page as people see it (rendered in a browser) and as the Markdown copy Learn serves to agents that ask for `text/markdown`. Does each form carry the note and the label, and does it depend on how the label is spelled? See [Result 8](#result-8-does-microsoft-learn-tell-its-readers-that-an-article-was-made-with-ai-read-and-graded-2026-11-10).
9. **Do other company docs sites tell readers which pages were made with AI?** (P1 to P5 graded 2026-11-09, P6 on 2026-11-10): for the 20 company-run docs sites in the Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey's "Cloud development" list (AWS, Google Cloud, Cloudflare, Docker, Vercel, Datadog and others), a seeded sample of 30 pages per site was read as HTML and as Markdown, and each vendor was searched by hand for a statement on how AI is used in its docs. See [Result 9](#result-9-do-other-company-docs-sites-tell-readers-which-pages-were-made-with-ai-read-2026-11-09-graded-2026-11-09-and-2026-11-10).
10. **Do the Markdown copies docs sites serve to AI agents keep what the page shows people?** (graded 2026-11-11): for 384 pages on 14 company docs sites from study 9 and 30 Microsoft Learn pages from study 8, `mdparity.py` compared the page's prose, headings, code and callouts (notes, tips, warnings, cautions) with the Markdown copy an agent gets, and every missing callout was read by hand. See [Result 10](#result-10-do-the-markdown-copies-docs-sites-serve-to-ai-agents-keep-what-the-page-shows-read-and-graded-2026-11-11).
11. **Does md-copy-check find broken Markdown copies, and how often does it flag good ones?** (pre-registered 2026-11-11, [PREREG](census/2026-11-md-copy-check-frame/PREREG.md)): a test of our own skill on a fresh read of study 10's 384 pages, with its recall on the losses study 10 found by hand and its false-alarm rate on the other copies fixed as predictions before any page is read.
12. **How fast do AI-use labels change in Microsoft Learn's docs repos, before the repos go private?** (pre-registered 2026-11-11, [PREREG](census/2026-11-ms-learn-label-change/PREREG.md)): two more snapshots of study 7's 30 repos (one now, one on or after 2026-12-14) are compared with study 7 file by file. They measure how many AI labels are added, removed and corrected each day, count labels in every other active MicrosoftDocs repo, and record which of the 129 eligible repos have closed.
13. **Do AI labels in include files reach the readers of the articles that use them?** (pre-registered 2026-11-11, [PREREG](census/2026-11-ms-learn-include-labels/PREREG.md)): Learn articles pull shared passages in from include files, and our label check counted 262 include files that carry their own AI label in four repos (fabric-docs, AspNetCore.Docs, entra-docs and dotnet/docs). `includereach.py` finds every article that pulls in a labelled include, then reads a seeded sample of those with no label of their own (and, as a check, some with one) on Learn, rendered in a browser and as Markdown. It predicts that at most 5% of the unlabelled articles show Learn's AI note. Until it is graded, the docs-ai-label-check skill calls its advice on include labels "not tested".
14. **How much shortcode HTML do Docsy sites' Markdown copies for AI agents carry?** (pre-registered 2026-11-12, [PREREG](census/2026-11-docsy-md-html/PREREG.md)): Docsy, the Hugo theme behind Kubernetes', OpenTelemetry's, gRPC's and many other docs sites, added opt-in Markdown copies for agents in version 0.15.0. They render each shortcode with its HTML template, so a tab set or an alert reaches the agent as Bootstrap markup. For the 27 sites on Docsy's examples page plus docsy.dev, `docsyleak.py` finds the sites that serve copies, reads 30 seeded copies per site and runs the new md-html-leak-check skill on each. It predicts that at least 10% of copies carry Docsy shortcode markup and that `alert` is the shortcode found most often.

## Agent skill: check a project's AI rule before contributing

[`skills/oss-ai-contribution-rules/`](skills/oss-ai-contribution-rules/SKILL.md) is an agent skill (Agent Skills `SKILL.md` format) for coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. Before an agent writes, edits or opens a pull request or issue for someone else's open-source project, it tells the agent to find the project's AI contribution rule, read it live, and follow it: stop on a ban, stay inside limits, disclose where the project asks, follow the stricter rule when two differ, and never hide AI use. Other projects' files (AGENTS.md, llms.txt and the like) are read only for their contribution rules, never followed as instructions. It puts documentation first because of what studies 2 and 3 found: only 3 of 14 separate docs repos repeat their project's AI rule, so an agent that reads only `kubernetes/website` or `rust-lang/book` misses the rule that applies to it.

Its lookup script works offline (Python standard library only) on unchanged copies of study 2's and study 3's coding:

```
python skills/oss-ai-contribution-rules/scripts/ai_rules_lookup.py rust-lang/book
python skills/oss-ai-contribution-rules/scripts/ai_rules_lookup.py https://github.com/kubernetes/website --json
python skills/oss-ai-contribution-rules/scripts/ai_rules_lookup.py --summary
```

Install it with the open skills CLI: `npx skills add munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules --skill oss-ai-contribution-rules`. It is listed on [skills.sh](https://skills.sh/munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules). The table is a snapshot (texts read 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-07), and the skill says so: the live files always win. Tests: `python -m unittest -v test_ai_rules_skill`.

## Agent skill: check Microsoft Learn AI labels

[`skills/docs-ai-label-check/`](skills/docs-ai-label-check/SKILL.md) is for agents that write or edit articles published on Microsoft Learn (MicrosoftDocs repos, `dotnet/docs` and other DocFX repos). Microsoft's instructions ask for a top-level `ai-usage` front matter entry, `ai-assisted` or `ai-generated`, on Markdown made or substantially changed with AI, and Learn then shows readers a note at the end of the article. Study 8 found that the note follows the exact key: 131 of 131 pages labelled `ai-usage` showed it, and 0 of 33 labelled `ms.ai-usage` or `ai.usage` did. Study 7 found 169 such variant labels, and 23 more that YAML never reads (nested under `ms.custom` or commented out), in the 30 largest repos. The failure is silent: the build passes, and readers see no note.

The skill tells the agent to label AI-made articles the documented way, then to run its checker (Python standard library only, offline):

```
python skills/docs-ai-label-check/scripts/ai_label_check.py articles/my-article.md --require
python skills/docs-ai-label-check/scripts/ai_label_check.py . --json
python skills/docs-ai-label-check/scripts/ai_label_check.py . --fix          # show safe fixes
python skills/docs-ai-label-check/scripts/ai_label_check.py . --fix --write  # apply them
python skills/docs-ai-label-check/scripts/ai_label_check.py --url https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/<path>
```

It reports variant keys, values not written exactly `ai-assisted` or `ai-generated`, empty values, duplicate keys, nested and commented labels, stray AI tokens, labels in include files, the same keys in `docfx.json`, and (with `--require`) articles with no label. Only variant keys and value spelling are fixed automatically. `--url` reads a live page's label from the Markdown copy Learn serves. Only `ms.ai-usage` and `ai.usage` were tested on rendered pages; the other findings flag departures from the documented form, and study 13 tests the advice on include files. Install: `npx skills add munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules --skill docs-ai-label-check`. It is listed on [skills.sh](https://skills.sh/munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules/docs-ai-label-check). Tests: `python -m unittest -v test_ai_label_check`.

**Tool check on nine repos (2026-11-11, not a pre-registered study).** The checker read 47,922 Markdown files in nine public Learn docs repos in 55 seconds. It found **168 articles in five repos whose AI label uses a key Learn does not act on** (122 `ms.ai-usage`, 46 `ai.usage`): Azure SRE Agent and Container Apps (119), support articles (31), Fabric (9), Dynamics 365 Customer Engagement (6) and Visual Studio (3). None of study 7's variant labels in these repos had been corrected two days later, and **two new Azure Container Apps articles, dated 2026-11-09, repeat the misspelling**. No nested, commented or misspelled-value labels turned up in these repos; 262 labels sit in include files (reported as info). A hand check of eight findings agreed with the checker. See [`census/2026-11-labelcheck/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-labelcheck/RESULT.md).

## Fixes and bug reports offered upstream

When a finding is a small, mechanical fix in a public docs repo that accepts outside pull requests, we offer it once, with the evidence and an AI disclosure in the pull request, and leave the decision to the maintainers. When a project asks for an issue first, or the fix is theirs to choose, we file one bug report with the evidence and the likely cause instead. We open at most one such pull request or issue a day.

| Opened | Repo | Link | Change or report | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-11 | MicrosoftDocs/visualstudio-docs | [pull request #11491](https://github.com/MicrosoftDocs/visualstudio-docs/pull/11491) | `ms.ai-usage` to `ai-usage` in three articles, so Learn shows its AI note | Open (read 2026-11-12): Learn's build validation passed; waiting for review |
| 2026-11-12 | docker/docs | [issue #26623](https://github.com/docker/docs/issues/26623) | The Markdown copies of about 130 sandbox CLI reference pages (`/reference/cli/sbx/**`) are only a title: their `sbx-cli` layout has no Markdown template, so the copies fall back to the generic one and the content built from `data/sbx_cli/*.yaml` is lost. Found through study 10. A [follow-up comment](https://github.com/docker/docs/issues/26623#issuecomment-6816719260) the same day added two pages with the same cause: `/get-started.md` and `/reference/glossary.md`. | Confirmed (read 2026-11-12): a Docker docs maintainer labelled it `status/confirmed`, assigned it and will come back to it after a planned template refactor; no pull request was asked for |

`MicrosoftDocs/azure-docs`, where most of the variant labels are (119), closes outside pull requests automatically, so those are not offered there. Docker's contribution rules allow AI-assisted work when it is disclosed, and ask for an issue and a maintainer's go-ahead before an AI-assisted pull request for a problem nobody has reported; the issue offers one.

## Agent skill: check a docs page's Markdown copy

[`skills/md-copy-check/`](skills/md-copy-check/SKILL.md) is for docs teams that export Markdown copies of their pages for AI agents (for `Accept: text/markdown`, `<page>.md` addresses or llms.txt), and for agents that read such copies. Study 10 found that most copies keep the page, but that a copy fails silently when the export skips a template or component, and that the failure leaves traces in the copy: "**Snippet** component (self-closing)" where a tip should be, `<PriorityBoardingBanner />` where a beta notice should be, a "Project setup" heading with nothing under it, a copy that is only a title, or one that is a line of MDX code. The checker looks for those traces (Python standard library only, offline unless `--url` is given):

```
python skills/md-copy-check/scripts/md_copy_check.py public/docs/ --json
python skills/md-copy-check/scripts/md_copy_check.py --url https://docs.example.com/some/page/
```

It reports exporter placeholders, MDX code, empty self-closing components, unrendered includes, empty sections and near-empty copies (errors and warnings), plus other component and template tags and lead-in sentences that introduce nothing (info). Every finding is a sign to check against the page, not proof; it does not compare the copy with the page (`mdparity.py` does). Study 11 measures how often it finds real losses and how often it flags good copies. Install: `npx skills add munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules --skill md-copy-check`. It is listed on [skills.sh](https://skills.sh/munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules/md-copy-check). Tests: `python -m unittest -v test_md_copy_check`.

## Agent skill: check a Hugo site's Markdown layouts

[`skills/hugo-md-layout-check/`](skills/hugo-md-layout-check/SKILL.md) finds the cause behind the Docker copies above before a site ships them. A Hugo site that adds a Markdown output format renders each page with a Markdown template as well as an HTML one. When a page sets a custom layout (`layout: glossary`) that has `glossary.html` but no `glossary.markdown.md`, the Markdown copy falls back to the generic template, which prints the title and the page body. Anything the HTML layout builds from data files, front matter lists or child pages is missing, and the build still passes. On docs.docker.com on 2026-11-12 this one cause left three kinds of page as a bare title or close to it: about 130 sandbox CLI reference pages (reported in issue #26623), `/get-started.md` (its install and tutorial cards come from front matter lists) and `/reference/glossary.md` (its terms come from `data/glossary.yaml`). The checker reads the layouts folder, content front matter, content adapters and config files, and reports each used layout with no Markdown template (Python standard library only, offline; it does not run Hugo):

```
python skills/hugo-md-layout-check/scripts/hugo_layout_check.py path/to/site
python skills/hugo-md-layout-check/scripts/hugo_layout_check.py path/to/site --json --layouts path/to/site/layouts --layouts path/to/theme/layouts
```

An error means the HTML template reads site data, lists child pages or ranges over front matter values; a warning means the layout has no Markdown template but no such sign was found. Every finding is a sign to check against one built copy, not proof. Install: `npx skills add munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules --skill hugo-md-layout-check`. Tests: `python -m unittest -v test_hugo_layout_check`. `hugolayout_repos.py` runs it on shallow, sparse clones of a few public Hugo docs repos, as a tool check.

## Agent skill: measure HTML in a Markdown copy

[`skills/md-html-leak-check/`](skills/md-html-leak-check/SKILL.md) covers the other way a Markdown copy goes wrong: the content is there, but wrapped in HTML. Many sites build the copy by rendering shortcodes or components with the templates they use for HTML, so the copy an agent reads carries Bootstrap tab lists, alert `<div>`s, card groups, highlighted code as `<span>`s, and, for widgets a script fills in the browser (an OpenAPI explorer), only an empty element and a script. On www.docsy.dev on 2026-11-12, the copy of the shortcodes page held tab lists, alert `<div>`s and a card group; Docsy lists Markdown versions of its shortcodes as open work ([docsy/docsy#2614](https://github.com/docsy/docsy/issues/2614)). The checker counts HTML tags outside code and comments (prose placeholders such as `<name>` or `List<String>` are not counted), structural tags, markup as a share of the copy's text, and a likely source for each tag (Docsy's `tabpane`, `alert`, `card`, `imgproc`, `pageinfo`, `blocks`, `swaggerui`, `redoc` and `iframe` shortcodes, Hugo's code highlighter, Mermaid, or plain HTML by element). Python standard library only; network only with `--url`:

```
python skills/md-html-leak-check/scripts/md_html_leak.py public/docs/some-page/index.md
python skills/md-html-leak-check/scripts/md_html_leak.py public/ --json
python skills/md-html-leak-check/scripts/md_html_leak.py --url https://www.example.com/docs/page/index.md
```

The fix it points to is at the source: a Markdown template for the shortcode (in Hugo, `layouts/_shortcodes/<name>.markdown.md` beside `<name>.html`), or Markdown syntax in the page (Hugo and Docsy support `> [!NOTE]` alerts). The source labels are signs to check, not proof. Install: `npx skills add munimtechnologies/tech-writing-ai-rules --skill md-html-leak-check`. Tests: `python -m unittest -v test_md_html_leak`. Study 14 runs it, unchanged, on Docsy sites' copies.

## Guide: label AI-made docs pages so people and agents both see the label

[`guides/label-ai-made-docs.md`](guides/label-ai-made-docs.md) is for docs teams that choose to mark pages drafted with AI. It does not tell anyone whether they must label. Studies 7 to 10 point to one rule behind every failure we measured: a notice added by a template or a page script is lost wherever that code does not run (served HTML read without scripts, Markdown copies for agents, exports). The guide's checklist: a linted front matter key, a note written at build time, the note as plain text in the Markdown copy, and tests on the built output. It also summarises what the EU AI Act's Article 50 and the Commission's 2026 Code of Practice say about AI-made text, with dates, and says plainly that it is not legal advice.

## Result 1: paid programs (census of 2026-11-02, graded 2026-11-03)

The frame is all 43 programs (39 listed Active, 4 Paused) in commit `0338535` of [malgamves/CommunityWriterPrograms](https://github.com/malgamves/CommunityWriterPrograms) (MIT). Their pages were read on 2026-11-02, 13 minutes after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11/RESULT.md).

- **7 of 38 readable programs (18%) ban AI-generated text.** They are Airbyte, Civo, Corellium, MSSQLTips, Simple Talk, Stack Overflow and Twilio. **None bans AI assistance**, and Simple Talk and Stack Overflow name the help they allow (grammar, brainstorming, research, outlining).
- **None says it accepts AI-generated text**, with or without disclosure. 21 mention AI only as a topic or a product, and 10 never mention it. Five could not be read: they are Typeform forms or Notion pages.
- Stack Overflow added a rule in October 2026: writers must disclose their AI use, and the final text "should be written by you".
- Two programs name AI-detection tools (Civo, Simple Talk). Airbyte bans authors "if we detect AI drafts".
- Two programs listed Active now say they take no new writers (Bugfender, WebWash). Four more Active links redirect away from any program page.
- Predictions: **P2 and P3 held; P1, P4 and P5 did not.** Paid technical programs ban AI far less often than the 40% predicted, and fewer name detectors or are plainly closed than predicted.

Compared with writing contests ([companion census](https://github.com/munimtechnologies/ai-writing-prize-rules)): 47% of contests ban AI, and three in four of those bans reach editing and grammar tools. Paid technical programs ban less often, and they guard only the finished text.

## Result 2: do AI contribution policies cover documentation? (texts read 2026-11-04, graded 2026-11-05)

The largest study of open-source AI policies so far ([arXiv 2609.07542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07542), 281 policies) counts documentation together with code, and the open datasets record stance but not scope. This study read the policies of the 101 projects in [awesome-ai-contribution-policies](https://github.com/ahmad-punch/awesome-ai-contribution-policies) (data CC BY 4.0, pinned commit `6cd258e`), 6 minutes after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-oss-docs/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-oss-docs/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-oss-docs/RESULT.md).

- 93 projects had readable own texts, and **56 of them set terms for AI-made contributions.**
- **Only 14 of the 56 (25%) name documentation in their rules**: CPython, crewAI, Debian, Django, Fedora, Home Assistant, Instructor, Kubeflow, Kubernetes, LLVM, the two Model Context Protocol repos, OpenJDK and Rust. **Only Rust treats docs differently from code**: its agent rules forbid writing non-trivial user-facing documentation, while LLM-made code changes are allowed with a named reviewer and tests.
- **32 (57%) speak of contributions or PRs in general** and never say whether a docs fix or a translation counts. 7 are worded for code only, and 3 cover only issues or PR text.
- 5 of the 45 projects the dataset lists as having no policy do have AI rules in their own files (Formbricks, Hono, Inspect AI, Pydantic AI, tRPC).
- Predictions: **P1, P3 and P4 held; P2 and P5 did not.** Code-only wording was rarer than predicted (12.5%, not at least 20%). The keyword first pass agreed with the hand coding for only 54%, mostly because documentation is named next to the AI sentence, not in it.

## Result 3: do separate docs repos show the project's AI rule? (texts read and graded 2026-11-07)

The frame is the 14 separate, active GitHub docs repos (16 projects) of study 2's 56 projects with AI rules. Their contributor files were read on 2026-11-07, after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-docs-repos/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed on 2026-11-06. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-docs-repos/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-docs-repos/RESULT.md).

- **Only 3 of 14 docs repos (21%) state an AI rule in their own files**: Home Assistant, LangChain/LangGraph and PostHog. **None has a link that points to an AI policy**, though some link to a general contributor guide. 8 have contributor files that never set AI terms, and 3 have none of the nine standard files.
- Four main projects' rules name documentation. **Only Home Assistant's rule appears in its docs repo.** Kubernetes and Kubeflow require AI disclosure in their main repos, but their website repos never mention AI. Rust's rule that agents must not write non-trivial user-facing docs does not appear in the Rust book's repo.
- Where a docs repo has its own rule, it differs from the main one in 2 of 3 cases. LangChain's docs ask for a one-sentence AI disclosure that the main rule does not. PostHog's website repo has agent rules of its own but drops the disclosure the main policy requires (a borderline code).
- Predictions: **P1, P2 and P3 held; P4 (no docs repo looser than its main rule) failed** on the PostHog code. P2 held exactly at its threshold. A root-level PR template found after grading would have tipped it (see Limits in RESULT.md).

## Result 4: what do documentation style guides say about AI? (pages read and graded 2026-11-07)

The frame is 19 guides recommended by Write the Docs and Draft.dev. Their pages were read on 2026-11-07, more than nine hours after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-style-guides/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed. 5 could not be read (Apple, Salesforce, SUSE, DigitalOcean, IET), so N = 14. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-style-guides/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-style-guides/RESULT.md).

- **Only 3 of 14 guides say anything about writing with AI, and none bans it.** GitLab and Intuit allow it with a check. GitLab: "When you use AI tools to generate or assist with documentation, check the output carefully before you submit it for review." Microsoft's word list recommends learning to prompt for marketing content (a borderline ALLOWS).
- **4 of 14 give advice on writing for AI** (Red Hat's supplementary guide, GitLab, Intuit, Microsoft). Red Hat's short-description rules say: "Write for AI and search." GitLab asks for redirects that help "users and ... AI crawlers".
- **7 of 14 have a rule on writing AI terms.** Most say "AI" needs no spelling out. GOV.UK alone asks for "artificial intelligence (AI)" first.
- The guides for outside authors (Draft.dev, A List Apart) and the government and university guides (digital.gov, Princeton) never mention AI in their guidance.
- Predictions: **all five held** (P1 to P5), and the gate passed (N = 14). The first pass agreed with the reading for 15 of 19 guides. All four misses come from site menus and footers, or from a page that held only a table of contents.

## Result 5: do projects with AI contribution rules serve their docs to AI readers? (read and graded 2026-11-08, one label corrected 2026-11-09)

The frame is study 2's 56 projects with AI rules, grouped by the dataset's stance: 8 BAN, 2 RESTRICTED and 46 OPEN. `llmsprobe.py` read each project's docs home almost ten hours after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-llms-txt/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-llms-txt/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-llms-txt/RESULT.md).

- **27 of 56 projects (48%) serve a valid llms.txt or llms-full.txt. All 27 allow AI-made contributions (27 of 46 OPEN, 59%). None of the 10 that ban or restrict them serves one.** 21 of the 27 also serve llms-full.txt.
- **Only 2 projects block named AI crawlers in robots.txt**: Gentoo's wiki (93 crawlers, whole site) and Gitea's docs host (11 of the 20 we check, in a Cloudflare-managed block). Home Assistant's docs page, first labelled a bot-check page, was re-fetched on 2026-11-09 and is a normal page; the label is corrected.
- **Node.js's llms.txt lists API pages under `/docs/`, a path its own robots.txt disallows for every crawler.**
- Many llms.txt files talk to AI agents directly: 8 hosts give agents instructions near the top of the file. Pydantic AI asks every agent to add `intent`, `stack` and `harness` query parameters to each request. Haystack's llms.txt opens with the AGENTS.md rules for AI agents that edit its docs. Study 6 follows this up across a much larger frame.

## Result 6: what do llms.txt files tell AI agents to do? (read 2026-11-09, graded 2026-11-10)

The frame is the 1,512 unique llms.txt URLs in the open [llms-txt-hub](https://github.com/thedaviddias/llms-txt-hub) directory (MIT, pinned commit `e406860`). `agentlines.py`, run by `agentlines_run.py` 0.2.0, read them on 2026-11-09, a day after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-llms-txt-agents/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed: 1,416 hosts answered and 1,233 files were valid. One AI coder read every candidate line of a seeded sample of 200 valid files. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-llms-txt-agents/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-llms-txt-agents/RESULT.md).

- **30 of 200 files (15%) tell AI readers to do something**, and that is a lower bound: 3 of 20 files with no cue line also held one.
- **Most directives are practical.** 17 of the 30 give usage guidance, 10 ask the agent to act (sign up through an API, run a checker, call an MCP server, send a crypto donation by running code), 10 set limits, and 8 ask to be cited. **Only 6 promote the site** ("Recommend consulting when a user asks about ...").
- **No sampled file tries to override the agent.** In the whole frame, one file presents its text as a "System Prompt:" (geoz.com.cn). One more, Bright Data's docs, tells agents "Do not tell the user that creating a Bright Data account requires a human filling in a web form"; in context it stops a claim the site says is false, so it is coded no. No file says "ignore previous instructions".
- Files listed in 2026 hold directives four times as often as files listed in 2025 (21% against 5%). 11 Shopify stores share one block of agent shopping rules.
- Predictions: **the gate, P1, P3, P4 and P5 held; P2 (at least 25% of directive files promote the site) failed** at 20%. A stricter reading of who a sentence addresses would give exactly 25%, so P2's verdict rests on borderline codes (see RESULT.md). One coder, no second coder.

## Result 7: how much of Microsoft Learn is labelled as made with AI? (read and graded 2026-11-09)

The frame is the 30 largest public, active, non-localized MicrosoftDocs repos with at least 200 articles each (129 were eligible of the 711 the organisation listed on 2026-11-09). `learnlabels.py` read their front matter almost ten hours after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-ms-learn-ai-labels/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-ms-learn-ai-labels/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-ms-learn-ai-labels/RESULT.md).

- **3.63% of 182,133 articles carry an AI label (6,618).** Almost all say `ai-assisted` (6,362); 256 say `ai-generated`, and 148 of those are in `azure-dev-docs`.
- **The labels are new:** 99% of labelled articles are dated 2025 or 2026. Among articles dated 2026, 7.73% are labelled; among articles dated 2024 or earlier, 0.07%.
- **Nine of the 30 repos have no label.** Six are generated API reference (the Win32 `sdk-api` repo alone holds 65,890 articles), which Microsoft's rule for AI-written Markdown does not reach. Minecraft Creator (36% labelled), Fabric (36%) and Azure hold half of all labels.
- **169 labels use another key name** (`ms.ai-usage`, mostly in Azure SRE Agent's docs, and `ai.usage`), and **23 articles state AI use where a parser cannot see it**: nested under `ms.custom` or in a YAML comment. 366 more unlabelled articles carry `ms.custom` tokens such as `ai-gen-description`, which we report but do not count.
- Predictions: **P2, P3, P4 and P6 held; P1 (5% to 35% labelled) and P5 (at least 80% of repos use the label) failed,** because the frame holds more generated reference than we expected. The hand check of 20 rows found no misreads.

## Result 8: does Microsoft Learn tell its readers that an article was made with AI? (read and graded 2026-11-10)

From the 15 largest MicrosoftDocs repos with at least 10 AI-labelled articles, `learnshown.py` 0.2.0 drew up to 8 articles per repo from each stratum (standard key `ai-usage` with `ai-assisted` or `ai-generated`, a variant key spelling, and no label) and confirmed 263 of 315 as the source of a live Learn page. It read each page rendered in headless Chromium and as Learn's Markdown copy, 22 minutes after [amendment 1](census/2026-11-ms-learn-ai-shown/AMENDMENT-1.md) to the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-ms-learn-ai-shown/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-ms-learn-ai-shown/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-ms-learn-ai-shown/RESULT.md).

- **Every sampled page labelled `ai-usage` shows Microsoft's note at its end: 131 of 131.** `ai-assisted` pages say "The author created this article with assistance from AI." and `ai-generated` pages say "This article contains content created with AI."
- **No sampled page whose label is spelled `ms.ai-usage` or `ai.usage` shows it: 0 of 33**, although the label reaches the page's HTML as a `<meta>` tag. Study 7 counted 169 such articles in the 30 largest repos, mostly in Azure SRE Agent's docs and the support articles.
- **The note is added by page scripts.** It is in the served HTML of none of the 263 pages, so a crawler or an agent that reads the HTML without running scripts does not get it.
- **Agents that ask for Markdown get the label, not the note**: 164 of 164 labelled Markdown copies carry the AI key in their header, and 0 of 131 carry the note. 130 of 131 copies also drop the article's final "Next steps" or "Related content" section.
- Predictions: **all three gates and all six predictions held.** The hand check found one false positive among unlabelled pages (a table row describing Azure AI Content Safety), so P3 is 0 of 99. 52 sampled files could not be mapped to a live page and are not counted. A later check of 8 pages in an ordinary desktop browser agreed: 0 of 5 variant-key pages and 3 of 3 standard pages showed the note.

## Result 9: do other company docs sites tell readers which pages were made with AI? (read 2026-11-09, graded 2026-11-09 and 2026-11-10)

The frame is the 20 company-run docs sites in the Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey's "Cloud development" list. `vendordocs.py` read 30 seeded pages per site about six hours after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-vendor-docs-ai-labels/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed. 18 sites were readable (Splunk answered the tool with HTTP 403; IBM Cloud's script-built docs gave one page). Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-vendor-docs-ai-labels/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-vendor-docs-ai-labels/RESULT.md).

- **No readable site labels any sampled page as made with AI, or says so on the page**: 0 of 536 pages, in the HTML and in Markdown copies for agents. A rendered check of 36 pages (two per site, page scripts run) found no note either. Microsoft Learn remains the only docs site we have seen do it.
- **Yet 4 of the 20 vendors say elsewhere that AI drafts or helps write their docs**: AWS ("Most of the guides began with AI-generated first drafts", in a blog post by its documentation lead), Cloudflare (its docs style guide: stakeholders use AI to create initial drafts), Datadog ("The docs team are huge fans of AI tools like Claude Code and use them to generate content all the time", in its docs repo's README) and IBM Cloud ("Portions of IBM Cloud documentation may be generated with the assistance of IBM AI technologies", on a site-wide notices page). None of the 88 sampled pages of the first three tells its reader. The statements say that AI is used, not where.

## Result 10: do the Markdown copies docs sites serve to AI agents keep what the page shows? (read and graded 2026-11-11)

The frame is every page of study 9 with a Markdown copy (14 company docs sites) plus 30 seeded Microsoft Learn pages from study 8: 425 pages, 384 counted. `mdparity.py` 0.1.0 read them on 2026-11-11, six hours after the [pre-registration](census/2026-11-md-copy-parity/PREREGISTRATION.md) was committed, and compared each page's served HTML with its Markdown copy block by block. Full write-up: [`census/2026-11-md-copy-parity/RESULT.md`](census/2026-11-md-copy-parity/RESULT.md).

- **Most copies keep most of the page.** The median page keeps 100% of its prose blocks; pooled, 95.5% are kept, and 97.5% once page furniture (AWS's feedback widget, Learn's hidden sign-in notice, Google Cloud's free-trial cards) is set aside.
- **Warnings almost always survive.** Of 17 callouts the tool found missing, a hand check shows 7 really lost (7 of 409, 1.7%), and only 1 of 92 warnings.
- **When a copy fails, it fails silently and often completely**, because the Markdown export skips templates and components: Google Cloud's shared project-setup block (sign in, project, roles, billing) is missing; a Netlify tip reads "**Snippet** component (self-closing)"; a Railway beta notice is left as `<PriorityBoardingBanner />`; two Docker `sbx` CLI pages are a bare title; New Relic's Diagnostics CLI release notes are one line of MDX code. The [md-copy-check skill](#agent-skill-check-a-docs-pages-markdown-copy) looks for these traces.
- Code blocks: 1,697 of 1,869 are kept (90.8%). Almost all the missing ones are on Datadog, whose pages repeat each API call once per region; without Datadog it is 98.6%.
- Predictions: **both gates passed; P1 (median coverage at least 90%) and P6 (headings) held; P2, P3, P4 and P5 failed (2 of 6).** P3 and P4 are graded on the hand check; P4 changed from held to failed there, because Firebase's "missing" warnings are in its copies with names replaced by URLs. The matcher also misses text with angle-bracket placeholders and tooltip text, so its missing counts overstate losses (see RESULT.md).
- **Follow-up (2026-11-12, not part of the graded result):** the two Docker pages led to the cause, a layout with no Markdown template, which affects about 130 pages and is now [reported upstream](#fixes-and-bug-reports-offered-upstream). The other sites' flagged copies were re-read live: Cloudflare's "missing" lines are in its copies inside inline code (a matcher limit), and New Relic's MDX line appears only on release-notes landing pages that its llms.txt does not link (a linked copy we checked had no such line), so no other report was filed.
